Reselling fees stack twice before the profit shows

A $300 thrift haul that sells for $900 is not a $600 profit. Reselling takes the fee twice: once when the item sells and again when the money lands in a bank account. A twelve percent marketplace commission on $900 is $108, and moving the payout out costs another $25 at a flat twenty-five cents a transfer. Under one percent of the haul, twenty-five dollars, buys nothing but the round trip. The $600 on paper arrives as $467 in the bank.

Buyer demand is a clock that runs while fees accrue. An item sitting ninety days at six percent monthly storage burns sixteen dollars while it waits, which quietly moves a break-even flip into a loser. Price cuts are the other half of the clock: dropping a listing twenty percent to force a sale surrenders $180 on a $900 item, more than every fee on the page combined.

The formula is four lines and no spreadsheet: payout, minus marketplace fee, minus payout transfer fee, minus the days-on-shelf storage cost. What survives that line is the real hourly rate, and dividing it by the hours including the drive to the bins is how flippers get honest with themselves.
